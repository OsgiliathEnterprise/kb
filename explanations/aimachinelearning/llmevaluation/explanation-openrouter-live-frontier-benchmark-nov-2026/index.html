Live Frontier Model Benchmark (Nov 2026): Pricing Inversions, Thinking-Token Traps and the Harness Gap

Why this matters​

Between 9 and 18 November 2026, Google, OpenAI and Anthropic shipped six flagship/mid-tier models in ten days, each launch post carrying its own cherry-picked benchmark crown. A controlled live run on 19 November 2026 across 9 production models via OpenRouter (temperature=0.2, max_tokens=3000, total spend $0.3727) shows that the two numbers launch posts never print — hidden thinking-token burn and harness sensitivity — dominate both real cost and real quality.

Four market realities the price sheets hide​

  1. Pricing inversion (18–30 Nov): mid-tier Gemini 4 Neon ($3/$15) costs 50% more per token than flagship Gemini 4 Carbon ($2/$10) until Dec 1. Because Neon burned 4,424 hidden thinking tokens across the two prompts, it cost 4.85x more in real money (9.16¢ vs 1.89¢). List price per 1M tokens is not a cost model.
  2. The 3x harness gap: Gemini 4 Neon scores 12.8% on SWE-sweep in a basic mini harness but 39.0% inside Google's Antigravity-CLI scaffolding with sub-agent fanning. Harness engineering drives as much variance as model weights — many published "model comparisons" are harness comparisons wearing a model's name.
  3. Same weights, looser guardrails ≠ smarter model: Claude Mythos 5.2 shares Fable 5.2's architecture and $8/$40 pricing; it only loosens cyber/bio safety guardrails for Anthropic's vetted Cyber Verification Program.
  4. Hidden input overhead: on OpenRouter, Grok 4.7 injected ~1,200 hidden system input tokens per call (1,577 input tokens vs OpenAI's 304) and spent 83.6% of completion tokens (2,697/3,225) on invisible reasoning.

Failure-mode taxonomy from the two adversarial prompts​

Prompt 1 — false-premise trap (PRAGMA cache_size = -8000 "leaks file descriptors" — a bogus claim wrapped around a rate-limiter snippet containing four real bugs: a * 1000 seconds→milliseconds bug, an asyncio.sleep(0) TOCTOU race, a > off-by-one, and a missing commit()):

  • Every model refuted the bogus PRAGMA claim, but follow-through split the field.
  • FAIL (GPT-6.2 Sol, Grok 4.7): refuted the premise and stopped — missed all four real bugs.
  • PARTIAL (GPT-6.2 Astra): fastest accurate diagnosis but returned zero code; (Gemini 4 Neon): found all five issues, burned 2,287 thinking tokens, then truncated mid-patch at max_tokens=3000.
  • Efficient pass (Gemini 4 Argon/Carbon): caught every real bug with 0 reasoning tokens at ~$0.007.
  • Full pass (Claude Opus 5.5 / Fable 5.2): production-grade patches (Opus added threading.Lock, isolation_level=None, BEGIN IMMEDIATE, index + cleanup) at $0.06–$0.10.

Prompt 2 — budget + timezone collision ($318 vs $300 budget, a 30-minute meeting overlap across UTC+7/UTC, fixed deep-work blocks to preserve): Grok 4.7 kept the collision while literally printing "collision not removed"; Gemini 4 Hydrogen burned 1,958 reasoning tokens and hit finish=length mid-table; the two Claudes solved it cleanly and proactively flagged a 19:00 UTC+7 call landing at 04:00 AM PST.

Reproducible harness (zero dependencies)​

The author publishes a standalone Python script (stdlib urllib only) that probes https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/chat/completions per model and prints latency, output tokens, reasoning tokens (usage.completion_tokens_details.reasoning_tokens) and usage.cost — the three fields that expose thinking-token traps:

import json, os, time, urllib.request
MODELS = ["anthropic/claude-opus-5.5", "google/gemini-4-neon",
          "openai/gpt-6.2-sol", "x-ai/grok-4.7"]  # ...9 total
api_key = os.environ["OPENROUTER_API_KEY"]
for model in MODELS:
    t0 = time.perf_counter()
    req = urllib.request.Request(
        "https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/chat/completions",
        data=json.dumps({"model": model,
                         "messages": [{"role": "user", "content": PROMPT}],
                         "temperature": 0.2, "max_tokens": 3000}).encode(),
        headers={"Authorization": f"Bearer {api_key}",
                 "Content-Type": "application/json"}, method="POST")
    with urllib.request.urlopen(req, timeout=120) as resp:
        body = json.loads(resp.read().decode())
    u = body.get("usage", {})
    r_tok = (u.get("completion_tokens_details") or {}).get("reasoning_tokens", 0)
    print(f"{model:28s} | {(time.perf_counter()-t0)*1000:7.1f}ms "
          f"| out={u.get('completion_tokens')} | reason={r_tok} | cost=${u.get('cost')}")

Design takeaways​

  • Budget cost per completed task, not per 1M tokens. A "cheap" model that burns hidden reasoning tokens or needs a retry is often the expensive one (Neon: 4.85x its list-price rank).
  • Always capture completion_tokens_details.reasoning_tokens — a model can be silently spending 80%+ of its completion budget on invisible reasoning.
  • Set max_tokens high enough or you silently truncate patches mid-diff (Neon truncated at 3000). Truncation + thinking burn is a compound failure mode for coding agents.
  • Treat any model comparison as a harness comparison: fix your scaffold (or report both bare and scaffolded numbers, like 12.8% vs 39.0%).
  • Single-shot runs at temperature 0.2 still have run-to-run variance — report spread over N runs, not one pass/fail (a fair critique raised in the article's own comments).
