🔴 P0 - Critical​

OpenSSL: Buffer overflow in EVP_EncodeBlock() (CVE-2026-121481, CVSS 9.8)​

A buffer overflow in OpenSSL's EVP_EncodeBlock() base64 encoding path — triggered by NULL input/output buffers, negative lengths, or lengths whose encoded size overflows an int — was disclosed with a public fix PR; the advisory is still pending and the OpenSSL SRT asked for the fix to be done publicly. The reporter demonstrated command-line exploitation by passing a very large -proxy_pass to openssl s_client (confirmed working against GOST via an environment-variable-supplied password); the pattern mirrors CVE-2021-23840. The proposed fix returns -1 on invalid input (matching wolfSSL's compatibility layer), adds a similar guard to EVP_DecodeBlock(), and corrects a faulty length check in http_client.c's base64encode(). Track the PR/advisory and patch as soon as a release ships. Source: OpenSSL PR #33652 · GHSA-83xj-7xh4-4p7x

🟠 P1 - Important​

PostgreSQL 19 released (2026-11-19)​

The headline feature is the new REPACK command: REPACK (CONCURRENTLY) reclaims table bloat and reorganizes rows while reads and writes continue (brief final lock, logical decoding to capture changes), replacing the risky VACUUM FULL/third-party pg_repack/pg_squeeze paths with a built-in online option. Also new: pg_plan_advice + pg_stash_advice extensions to control/automatically stabilize query plans, automatic scaling of I/O worker processes, quicker foreign-key checks, logical replication enablement without restart (when wal_level=replica), replication of sequence values, "read-your-writes" on standbys via the new WAIT command, and parallel autovacuum of indexes with a prioritization scoring system. Migration requires pg_dumpall/pg_upgrade/logical replication; Databricks shipped Postgres 19 on Lakebase the same day, and Snowflake's engineering post is a good practical walkthrough of bloat diagnostics (pg_stat_user_tables dead-tuple ratios) and REPACK vs pg_repack. 19.1 is anticipated in February — a sensible production-upgrade target. Sources: Release notes · Snowflake: Postgres 19 REPACK · Databricks: Postgres 19 on Lakebase

Gemini 4 Neon tops the Models Engineering Index​

Google's Gemini 4 Neon — DeepMind's fourth model in seven weeks — is #1 on the Models Engineering Index at 68.0, ahead of Anthropic's Fable 5.2 (65.3), OpenAI's GPT-6.2 Astra (61.5) and Opus 5.5 (61.1), at 1.6× reference cost. Developpez's report adds Artificial Analysis numbers: 61 on the intelligence index (+1 vs Gemini 4 Carbon), $6.44 per task (~17% cheaper than Carbon's $7.76) in ~21 min (~40% faster), and 59 on the Coding Agent Index (vs Carbon's 55), putting it in Claude Fable 5.2/Opus 5.5 territory on coding at notably lower cost. Sources: Models Engineering Index · Developpez

JetBrains Rider 2026.3 released alongside .NET 11​

Rider 2026.3 ships with .NET 11 support and JetBrains Air (agentic AI development experience), with a strong game-dev focus: deeper Godot integration, improved Unreal Engine and custom C++ engine debugging, Unity CoreCLR player support, and new beta Lua and Verse language plugins (Lua for scripting alongside C++/C#). Source: Developpez

🟡 P2 - Interesting​

GIMP now has real UI tests​

GIMP added a GUI testing platform that starts a real GIMP in a hidden virtual desktop, clicks menus/buttons, and verifies results — running automatically in CI on every merge. The 13 initial tests cover startup, dialogs, File > New/Export, layers, undo/redo, zoom, toolbox, preferences and menu actions; crucially, tests locate widgets via accessibility information (the same data screen readers use), so improving testability also improves accessibility, and the team contributed a headless-mode feature upstream to dogtail (Linux/X11+Wayland only for now). Source: GIMP news

I Measured My MCP Server's Context Tax Before Adding a Third Tool​

A grounded, measured take on the "MCP context tax": every registered tool's full JSON schema is sent with every request whether used or not, so it's a standing charge per turn. The author's 30-line audit script found his two-tool server costs ~200 tokens/turn (~0.1% of a 200k window) — near-zero, but he notes prompt caching reduces dollar cost, not attention cost, so schema bloat still competes with real work. Useful methodology for anyone about to stack several MCP servers. Source: DEV.to

Vint Cerf on why TCP/IP was never patented​

João Tomé launches his Future Stories podcast with Vint Cerf: the 83-year-old (retired from Google this year, now helping start Tolero for interplanetary networking — "the speed of light is too slow") explains that TCP/IP was deliberately opened up because they couldn't predict who the allies would be in 25 years — "the only way we can be assured that the allies will have access to this is to simply open it up." Also great anecdotes: Steve Crocker finding Cerf's two-week unreproducible bug in 30 seconds, and how CERFnet got its name. Source: seremot.com

Stop Renting Cloud GPUs & VPS: 4 Legitimate Free Compute Tiers in 2026​

A practical inventory of official free compute: Kaggle GPU (30 hrs/week P100 or dual-T4, no credit card, 12h session cap), Cloudflare Workers AI (10k Neurons/day for serverless LLMs/Whisper/embeddings, no card), Oracle Cloud Always Free (4 ARM OCPU + 24GB RAM forever, but strict card + residential-IP fraud checks), and GCP's $300/90-day trial that halts billing rather than auto-charging. Good cheat-sheet for zero-budget fine-tuning and cron workloads. Source: DEV.to

Fiona the Hippo Died After Eating a Hat. But How Does a Hat Kill a Hippo?​

Cincinnati Zoo's famous hippo Fiona (9) died from complications after ingesting a knit hat that flew through a one-foot gap above the viewing window after hitting a ceiling fan. A vet explains the mechanics: a hippo's intestine is likely 100+ feet long, so a stuck hat can cause swelling that cuts off blood flow → tissue death → leakage of gut contents → toxic shock; surgery on a hippo's abdomen is rarely viable, and if the hat hadn't passed within ~5 days, outcomes were grim. Source: WIRED
