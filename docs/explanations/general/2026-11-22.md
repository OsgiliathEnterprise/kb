## 🔴 P0 - Critical

### Together.ai discloses prompt-leakage security incident (June–July 2026)
Together AI notified customers that an unauthorized party downloaded stored prompt samples after an AI coding agent committed a credential for a private Hugging Face repository into its public GitHub repo (`xorl`). The samples came from serverless requests to MiniMax M3, GLM-5.2, and Kimi K2.6, primarily on June 24, July 4, 8, and 14; the attacker then searched them for credentials to other services and tested their validity (five were Dragos canary credentials). Together has engaged forensics and law enforcement, reverted the commit, and moved to scoped/short-lived credentials with PR credential scanning — if you used those models on Together's serverless tier in that window, treat prompts as potentially exposed.
Source: [Together.ai blog](https://www.together.ai/blog/security-event-prompt-leakage-for-requests-in-june-and-july-of-2026)

### Java4Shell, NGINX2Root, and PHP security releases: what 58M crawled sites actually run
A search-engine operator walks through three pre-auth RCE disclosures in three days: Java4Shell (CVE-2026-114938, OpenJDK Base64 decoder memory corruption, CVSS 8.1 Oracle / 9.2 Calif, pre-auth RCE against Keycloak, Jira, Confluence, Jenkins, Artifactory in default configs), NGINX2Root (five nginx flaws incl. two rated 9.2, fixed in nginx 1.30.7 / 1.31.9), and PHP 8.6.0 plus security releases 8.5.13 / 8.4.28 / 8.3.37 / 8.2.36 headed by the CVE-2026-115560 session-serializer RCE. Their crawl data (~58.2M domains) shows nginx on ~6.8M sites — the #2 server behind Cloudflare — so NGINX2Root's blast radius is millions of sites even with no in-the-wild exploitation yet. Practical takeaway: check your own `Server` header — advertising a version string hands attackers a free target shortlist.
Source: [DEV.to](https://dev.to/cl0qsearch/java4shell-nginx2root-and-a-php-security-release-what-58-million-crawled-sites-actually-run-47b6)

## 🟡 P2 - Interesting

### Toyota, Nissan, and Honda to standardize parts to cut costs
Japan's three largest automakers agreed via the Japan Automobile Manufacturers Association (chaired by Toyota vice chairman Koji Sato) to begin standardizing vehicle parts — starting with unified steel quality criteria, consistent plastics/resins protocols, and universal wiring-harness color coding. The initial targets are invisible-to-consumer, high-cost areas chosen to avoid diluting brand identity; expansion to whole-vehicle components is planned, with international suppliers to be included after domestic ones. A notable industry-collaboration shift for Japanese auto supply chains.
Source: [Car and Driver](https://www.caranddriver.com/news/a74490092/toyota-nissan-honda-standardize-parts-lower-costs/)

### Consentinel: a 12KB open-source fix for cookie banners leaking trackers before consent
The author argues most CMP banners (Cookiebot, OneTrust: 100–200KB+ JS, $15–500/mo) still let GTM-injected scripts, Meta Pixel, and `sendBeacon()` beacons fire before consent, because they only render a UI bar. Their alternative, Consentinel, is a GPL-2.0, ~12KB-gzipped pre-consent blocker that quarantines tracker `<script>` tags via MutationObserver, intercepts fetch/XHR/sendBeacon, defaults Google Consent Mode v2 storage categories to `denied`, and ships zero-CLS UI plus Magento 2/Next.js/Webflow installs. Worth a look as a lightweight CMP experiment, though "open-core with a Pro tier" claims warrant independent verification.
Source: [DEV.to](https://dev.to/rabig3015ai/why-most-cookie-banners-still-leak-gtm-meta-pixel-before-consent-and-a-12kb-open-source-fix-2835)
